## Introduction

The Drumelzier tradition says that Merlin was buried on the haugh beside the River Tweed, just north of Drumelzier church in historic [Peeblesshire]({{ 'where-peeblesshires-legends-still/' | relative_url }}). It is not a simple “wizard’s grave” story in the modern fantasy sense. The older evidence points instead to Lailoken, a prophetic wild man of the early medieval north, later folded into the wider Merlin tradition. The evidence trail is unusually rich for a local legend: medieval writing, an early modern antiquarian note, old map marking, place-name clues, river prophecy, and recent community archaeology all converge on the same small Tweedside landscape. None of this proves that Merlin, as popularly imagined, lies in the field. What it does show is that Drumelzier’s legend is old, locally rooted, and central to Peeblesshire’s identity as one of Scotland’s most compelling Arthurian landscapes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dark-age-digs.com/investigations/merlins-grave/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dark-age-digs.com">[Dark Age Digs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dark-age-digs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dark Age Digs Merlin’s Grave</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dark Age Digs Merlin’s Grave</span></span></span>

## The Drumelzier Death Legend

In the Drumelzier version, Merlin is not primarily King Arthur’s court magician. He is closer to Lailoken: a traumatised seer, connected with battle, madness, prophecy, captivity and a violent death beside the Tweed. The tale places him under the power of Meldred, a local ruler associated with Drumelzier, and links his end to the Powsail Burn and the River Tweed. The modern village is still framed by the same features that make the story feel so place-bound: the church, the burn, the river haugh, the ford area, and the hill of Tinnis Castle above the valley.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

The story’s most memorable episode is Merlin’s “triple death”. In later retellings he is attacked near the river, falls or is driven down, is wounded or impaled, and drowns with his head in the water. Scotland Starts Here, a modern South of Scotland visitor resource, gives the accessible heritage version: Merlin is ambushed as he crosses towards Drumelzier, after exposing the queen’s affairs, and dies by a combination of beating, falling onto a fish-trap stake and drowning in the Tweed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scotlandstartshere.com/point-of-interest/merlins-grave/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scotlandstartshere.com">[Scotland Starts Here]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scotlandstartshere.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Scotland Starts Here Merlin&#x27;s Grave &#124; History &amp; Heritage</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Scotland Starts Here Merlin&#x27;s Grave &#124; History &amp; Heritage</span></span></span>

That dramatic ending matters because it is not random gothic decoration. A threefold or “triple” death is a known legendary pattern, and the archaeological discussion around Drumelzier has noted that the motif sits beside other old elements in the tale, including prophecy, a wild seer figure, and possible pre-Christian overtones. The important point for a reader is not that every detail is literal, but that the death scene is a piece of traditional narrative architecture attached to a precise Peeblesshire riverscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

The grave itself is traditionally placed on the east bank haugh north of Drumelzier church. Dark Age Digs, the public site for the Drumelzier’s Hidden Heritage project, notes that the location has been marked on maps from 1775 onwards and is recorded in Scotland’s National Record of the Historic Environment, although no actual remains had previously been recorded at the reputed spot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dark-age-digs.com/investigations/merlins-grave/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dark-age-digs.com">[Dark Age Digs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dark-age-digs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dark Age Digs Merlin’s Grave</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dark Age Digs Merlin’s Grave</span></span></span>

## Why This Is More Than a Tourist Tale

The strongest case for Drumelzier is not a single artefact or a single document. It is the way different kinds of evidence gather around one landscape. The medieval story names or implies a place called Dunmeller, from which Drumelzier is derived; it refers to a meeting of waters, which fits the Powsail Burn and Tweed; and it places the action under a stronghold identified in local tradition with Tinnis Castle. Current Archaeology’s account of the GUARD-led project stresses that the question was whether a medieval storyteller had simply attached Merlin to a convenient scenery, or whether the legend had deeper Tweeddale roots.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

The name Lailoken is especially important. Current Archaeology reports that it is derived from Cumbric, the Brittonic language once spoken across southern Scotland in the early medieval period. That does not prove a historical Lailoken died at Drumelzier, but it does make the tale harder to dismiss as a late romantic invention. It points towards an older northern British story-world, later absorbed into the more famous Merlin tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

The medieval context also fits a wider historical horizon. The tale is associated with St Kentigern, the sixth-century bishop of Glasgow, and with the Battle of Arthuret, placed in the Welsh Annals in AD 573. Tim Clarkson’s study of Scotland’s Merlin, summarised by Google Books, follows the same wider pattern: Lailoken is treated as a sixth-century wild man of the Scottish Lowlands whose story later reappears in Welsh literature as Myrddin before becoming part of the Merlin known from Arthurian legend.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.co.uk/books?id=oU4kDAAAQBAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.co.uk">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Google Books Scotland&#x27;s Merlin: A Medieval Legend and its Dark Age Origins</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Books Scotland&#x27;s Merlin: A Medieval Legend and its Dark Age Origins</span></span></span>

For Peeblesshire, this makes Drumelzier unusually valuable. Many counties have haunted inns, witch stories or fairy wells, but Drumelzier has a legend where medieval text, local topography and modern archaeological questions can be followed almost step by step across a valley floor.

## Medieval Sources and Antiquarian Memory

The Drumelzier tradition is usually traced through a chain of written memory rather than through one clean “first source”. The key medieval work is the *Vita Merlini Silvestris*, or “Life of Merlin of the Forest”, a medieval tale probably written in Glasgow in the twelfth century, though surviving in a later copy. GUARD Archaeology describes the Drumelzier association as including Merlin’s imprisonment by a Dark Age king, his killing, and burial by the banks of the Tweed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.guard-archaeology.co.uk/GALNews/?p=655" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guard-archaeology.co.uk">[guard-archaeology.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guard-archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on guard-archaeology.co.uk.</span></span></span>

Walter Bower’s fifteenth-century *Scotichronicon* then preserves the story within Scotland’s great medieval chronicle tradition. Dark Age Digs notes that the story was included by Bower in the 1440s, helping to carry the Drumelzier association from medieval narrative into later Scottish historical memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dark-age-digs.com/merlins-grave-drumelzier-and-the-merlin-connection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dark-age-digs.com">[Dark Age Digs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dark-age-digs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dark Age Digs Merlin&#x27;s Grave</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dark Age Digs Merlin&#x27;s Grave</span></span></span>

The early modern link is Alexander Pennecuik, the physician and antiquarian whose work on Tweeddale preserves many details of Peeblesshire’s local memory. Current Archaeology quotes Pennecuik’s 1689 draft account, in which he says that the famous prophet Merlin was said to be buried “a little below the church yard”, and that the particular place had been shown to him years earlier by the old minister of the parish. This is a crucial moment in the evidence trail because it shows the grave as a local site pointed out by living memory, not merely a name in a literary text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

By the eighteenth century, the grave had entered cartographic memory. Current Archaeology notes that Merlin’s Grave was marked at Drumelzier on Mostyn Armstrong’s map of 1775. That map evidence does not prove a burial, but it does show that the tradition had become fixed enough in the landscape to be mapped as a recognised place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

The older official inventory tradition was cautious. The Royal Commission inventory for Peeblesshire speculated that the tradition might have begun with the discovery of a Bronze Age cist grave, but no record of such a find survives. This is a good example of how the evidence should be read: the antiquarian and map record is real, but the physical origin of the grave tradition remains uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://the-past.com/feature/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale-merlins-grave-and-other-lost-stories-embedded-in-the-landscape/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: the-past.com">[The Past]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">the-past.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Past Unearthing ancient Tweeddale: &#x27;Merlin&#x27;s Grave&#x27; and other</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Past Unearthing ancient Tweeddale: &#x27;Merlin&#x27;s Grave&#x27; and other</span></span></span>

## The River Prophecy

One reason the Drumelzier story survived so strongly is that it is not only a death legend. It is also a prophecy tale. The remembered saying runs in various forms: when Tweed and Powsail meet at Merlin’s grave, Scotland and England will have one monarch. Destination Tweed’s modern heritage account says this was linked in local tradition to 1603, when James VI of Scotland became James I of England and the river was said to have flooded into the burn at Drumelzier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://destinationtweed.org/story/merlin-the-man-the-myth-the-magic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: destinationtweed.org">[Destination Tweed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">destinationtweed.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Destination Tweed Merlin</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Destination Tweed Merlin</span></span></span>

This prophecy works as folklore because it binds a national event to a local hydrological image. The Union of the Crowns becomes something the landscape itself appears to confirm: two waters meeting at the prophet’s grave as two kingdoms come under one king. Whether the flood story can be independently verified is less important than what the tradition does. It turns a bend of the Tweed into a stage on which Peeblesshire participates in the history of Scotland and England.

The prophecy also helps explain why local exactness matters. A vague “Merlin was buried somewhere in Scotland” would be much weaker. Drumelzier’s story depends on the meeting of specific waters, a particular churchyard, a remembered haugh, and a grave location that could be shown, mis-shown, mapped, moved in memory, or debated.

## Archaeology, Absence and the Grave-Like Feature

Recent archaeology has made the Drumelzier legend more interesting, but it has not produced a simple headline of “Merlin found”. The Drumelzier’s Hidden Heritage project was a collaboration involving the Arthur Trail Association, local heritage groups, volunteers, Magnitude Surveys and GUARD Archaeology, with work designed to test whether the local legend had archaeological roots.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.guard-archaeology.co.uk/GALNews/?p=655" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guard-archaeology.co.uk">[guard-archaeology.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guard-archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on guard-archaeology.co.uk.</span></span></span>

The reputed grave site itself produced a careful negative result. In November 2022, volunteers and archaeologists carried out geophysical survey in the field north of Drumelzier. Current Archaeology reports that the spot marked as Merlin’s Grave showed no buried archaeological remains. That matters: a responsible evidence trail must include absence, not just attractive possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

The same survey, however, detected a large pit a short distance to the south-east of the marked site. The team could not excavate it, so it remains uncertain whether it is a grave, a pit of another kind, or something unrelated. Current Archaeology describes it as possible buried archaeological remains and explicitly notes that only excavation could determine whether it contained human remains or what date it belonged to.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

This is the most honest reading: the marked grave has no known structural remains; a nearby anomaly may be relevant; and the wider landscape has produced more substantial early medieval evidence. The value of the 2022 work is not that it solved the legend, but that it shifted the question from “is this just a made-up tourist spot?” to “what kind of early medieval landscape gave rise to such a persistent local tradition?”

## Tinnis Castle and the Wider Tweedside Setting

The grave cannot be understood apart from Tinnis Castle, the prominent hill site overlooking Drumelzier. In the legend, the ruler Meldred’s stronghold is part of the story-world in which Lailoken is held captive and prophecy is extracted from him. Current Archaeology notes that Tinnis Castle is commonly identified with Meldred’s “lofty throne” and that the place-name and river clues in the medieval tale align with Drumelzier’s landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

There is also a later medieval castle history here. Historic Environment Scotland’s scheduled monument record for Drumelzier Castle describes the remains as part of a complex medieval and late medieval fortified landscape, linked to the Tweedies of Drumelzier and to nearby fortifications including Tinnis Castle and Wrae Castle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505%3A300%3A%3A%3A%3A%3AVIEWTYPE%2CVIEWREF%3Adesignation%2CSM3149" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: portal.historicenvironment.scot">[Historic Environment Scotland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">portal.historicenvironment.scot</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on historicenvironment.scot.</span></span></span>

That later castle record should not be confused with proof of a sixth-century Merlin. Instead, it helps show why this valley kept attracting stories about power, lordship and strongholds. The landscape was not an empty stage. It was a real corridor of authority, settlement, routeways and fortified places, later remembered through both family history and legend.

The Drumelzier’s Hidden Heritage project framed its work in exactly this way. It asked not only whether there were features near Merlin’s Grave, but also how Tinnis Castle hillfort was dated, occupied and connected to other early medieval sites in Peeblesshire and the Scottish Borders. Its stated aim was to establish the basis of an early medieval monumental landscape in Tweeddale, a region where little archaeology from this period had previously been examined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dark-age-digs.com/drumelziers-hidden-heritage/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dark-age-digs.com">[Dark Age Digs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dark-age-digs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dark Age Digs Drumelzier’s Hidden Heritage</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dark Age Digs Drumelzier’s Hidden Heritage</span></span></span>

## What the Evidence Can and Cannot Prove

The evidence for Merlin’s Grave is best understood in layers.

First, there is strong evidence for an old Drumelzier tradition. Medieval sources, Pennecuik’s seventeenth-century note, eighteenth-century mapping, and modern local memory all place Merlin or Lailoken’s death and burial in this valley.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dark-age-digs.com/merlins-grave-drumelzier-and-the-merlin-connection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dark-age-digs.com">[Dark Age Digs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dark-age-digs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dark Age Digs Merlin&#x27;s Grave</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dark Age Digs Merlin&#x27;s Grave</span></span></span>

Second, there is good evidence that the tradition fits the landscape rather than floating above it. The Powsail Burn, the Tweed, Dunmeller or Drumelzier, and Tinnis Castle all give the tale a local structure. That does not prove every event, but it does show why the story belongs here rather than being interchangeable with any picturesque Scottish riverbank.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

Third, there is suggestive but not decisive archaeological evidence. The marked grave site has not yielded remains; a nearby pit-like anomaly may be relevant but is unexcavated; and the wider project has strengthened the case that Drumelzier sat within an early medieval landscape capable of generating or preserving such a story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archaeology.co.uk/articles/features/unearthing-ancient-tweeddale.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeology.co.uk">[Current Archaeology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeology.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Current Archaeology Unearthing Ancient Tweeddale</span></span></span>

Finally, there is no proof that the Arthurian Merlin of later romance was buried at Drumelzier. The better claim is subtler and more interesting: Drumelzier preserves one of the strongest local traditions for Lailoken, a northern prophetic wild man who helped feed the later Merlin legend. Clarkson’s modern study frames this as a transformation from Lailoken to Myrddin and then to the Merlin recognised from literature and screen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.co.uk/books?id=oU4kDAAAQBAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.co.uk">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Google Books Scotland&#x27;s Merlin: A Medieval Legend and its Dark Age Origins</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Books Scotland&#x27;s Merlin: A Medieval Legend and its Dark Age Origins</span></span></span>

## How the Story Is Retold Today

Today, Merlin’s Grave sits between folklore, archaeology and heritage tourism. Visitors are directed to the Drumelzier site by regional heritage material, while the Merlin Trail and Drumelzier’s Hidden Heritage project have encouraged people to read the valley as a connected storyscape rather than as a single isolated marker. Scotland Starts Here even warns visitors not to be confused by a plaque in the wrong place, which is a useful reminder that folklore sites often have a living geography of signs, memories and corrections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scotlandstartshere.com/point-of-interest/merlins-grave/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scotlandstartshere.com">[Scotland Starts Here]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scotlandstartshere.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Scotland Starts Here Merlin&#x27;s Grave &#124; History &amp; Heritage</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Scotland Starts Here Merlin&#x27;s Grave &#124; History &amp; Heritage</span></span></span>

Modern retellings also tend to restore the darker Merlin. The popular image of the wise bearded magician is still present, but Drumelzier’s version is stranger and more local: a fugitive prophet, a captive at a hillfort, a scandal at a petty court, a violent river death, and a grave tied to the meeting of waters. This is why the legend matters within Peeblesshire. It is not simply borrowed Arthuriana. It is a way of reading the upper Tweed as a place where medieval literature, early medieval memory, local place-names and modern fieldwork meet.

The result is a legend with unusual staying power. It can be enjoyed as a mysterious grave story, followed as a walking route, tested as an archaeological question, or read as part of the wider northern Merlin tradition. Its power lies in the tension between evidence and uncertainty: enough evidence to make Drumelzier far more than a fanciful label, not enough to flatten the tale into settled fact.

      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
