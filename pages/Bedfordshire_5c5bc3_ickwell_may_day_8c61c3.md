## Introduction

Ickwell May Day is [Bedfordshire]({{ 'where-bedfordshire-keeps-its-oldest/' | relative_url }})’s best-known living village custom: a May Bank Holiday procession from Northill to Ickwell Green, the crowning of a May Queen, children and adults dancing around the maypole, decorated hoops, Morris dancing, and the comic “Moggies” linked with old chimney-sweep May customs. Its importance is not just that it looks old, but that it has kept adapting. The records reach back to the Northill churchwardens’ accounts of 1563, while the present form owes much to nineteenth-century revival, local schools, the permanent maypole put up in 1872, and the organising work of the Ickwell and District May Day Committee after 1945.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk/CommunityHistories/Ickwell/IckwellMayDay.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk">[Bedfordshire Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hosted By Bedford Borough Council: Ickwell May Day...</span></span></span>

That makes Ickwell unusually valuable in Bedfordshire folklore. It is not a ghost story preserved in print, nor a custom reconstructed from a museum label. It is a repeated public act, tied to a particular green, road, parish and set of families. The village still dances because each generation has found a practical way to inherit the custom without freezing it in one imagined “ancient” form.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

## The maypole on Ickwell Green

The centre of Ickwell May Day is the maypole on Ickwell Green. Bedfordshire Archives puts the point plainly: Ickwell is renowned for its maypole, which stands on the village green and forms the focus of the May Day celebrations. That setting matters. The custom is not simply “in Bedfordshire” in a general sense; it is anchored in a small landscape of Northill church, the road to Ickwell, the green, the maypole arena, nearby cottages, Ickwell Bury and the wider parish villages that feed dancers and attendants into the festival.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk/CommunityHistories/Ickwell/IckwellMayDay.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk">[Bedfordshire Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hosted By Bedford Borough Council: Ickwell May Day...</span></span></span>

The official festival programme still preserves this spatial rhythm. The day begins at The Crown in Northill, where Morris men dance, children gather with decorated hoops, the hoops are judged, and the procession is assembled. At 1.30 pm the procession leaves for Ickwell Green, a half-mile walk along a road closed to traffic for the occasion. At the green, stalls and refreshments surround the arena, and the May Queen is crowned before children and adults from within the parish dance around the pole.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

This is one reason the festival feels more like a village rite than a general spring fair. It has a route. It has a centre. It has repeated roles. The green is not just a backdrop; it is the stage on which Ickwell becomes itself for the day. CPRE Bedfordshire notes that Ickwell Green has a maypole dating back to 1872 and also a long cricket history, showing how the green has served as a shared village space for both ritual and recreation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cprebeds.org.uk/discover/v-is-for-village-greens-and-common-land/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cprebeds.org.uk">[CPRE Bedfordshire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cprebeds.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cprebeds.org.uk.</span></span></span>

The permanent maypole is itself a relatively modern layer in an older custom. Bedfordshire Archives records that before 1872, poles had been put up on the night before May Day and taken down at the end of the day. In 1872, John Harvey of Ickwell Bury had a larch from Warden Warren erected on the green; a newspaper account said it stood 67 feet high, four feet around at the base, and was set in six feet of cement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk/CommunityHistories/Ickwell/IckwellMayDay.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk">[Bedfordshire Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hosted By Bedford Borough Council: Ickwell May Day...</span></span></span>

That shift from temporary tree to permanent landmark changed the character of the custom. A May pole brought in overnight belongs to a one-day burst of seasonal licence. A permanent maypole on the green turns the custom into an everyday village emblem. People can see it in January, walk past it in October, and still know that its real time is May. In folklore terms, that is how a seasonal ritual becomes a place-marker.

## How old is the Ickwell custom?

The oldest firm evidence comes from the parish of Northill’s sixteenth-century churchwardens’ accounts. Bedfordshire Archives identifies a detailed account dated 19 May 1563, listing expenses “for all our Maye”. The purchases included spice and fruit for baked meats, hops for beer, wheat, three calves, a minstrel, gunpowder, Morris coats, shoes for the Morris dancers and Morris bells.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk/CommunityHistories/Ickwell/IckwellMayDay.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk">[Bedfordshire Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hosted By Bedford Borough Council: Ickwell May Day...</span></span></span>

That is strong evidence for a substantial May celebration in the parish, but it also shows why careful wording matters. The archive explicitly says the account does not state whether the festivities were held in Northill, Ickwell, or both. So the safest claim is not that the whole modern Ickwell festival has continued unchanged since 1563. The safer and more interesting claim is that the Northill-Ickwell May custom has a documented sixteenth-century root, and that later generations attached it ever more clearly to Ickwell Green and its maypole.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk/CommunityHistories/Ickwell/IckwellMayDay.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk">[Bedfordshire Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hosted By Bedford Borough Council: Ickwell May Day...</span></span></span>

The documentary trail then becomes thinner. Bedfordshire Archives says it holds no further documents about May Day in Ickwell until John Harvey’s will, dated 1873, in which he left £2 10s to the Northill churchwardens to be spent each year on keeping up May Day at Ickwell “as has been done during my life”. Harvey died in 1879, and the will was proved that year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk/CommunityHistories/Ickwell/IckwellMayDay.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk">[Bedfordshire Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hosted By Bedford Borough Council: Ickwell May Day...</span></span></span>

That phrase is important because it points to remembered continuity even where surviving paperwork is sparse. It implies that May Day at Ickwell was already an established local practice in Harvey’s lifetime, not an invention created by the bequest. Yet it also leaves a gap between the sixteenth-century account and the nineteenth-century revival evidence. Ickwell’s power lies partly in that tension: it is old enough to be deeply rooted, but the precise line of survival is not a clean, unbroken chain visible in the archive.

## May Queens, Moggies and older May customs

Ickwell May Day combines several layers of English May tradition: greenery, procession, music, Morris dancing, a maypole, a May Queen, decorated hoops and comic figures. The result can look timeless, but its parts come from different periods and have been reshaped for village use.

The May Queen is now one of the most visible figures in the festival. The official history says that Ickwell’s newer style of celebration came in during the 1880s and 1890s, influenced by John Ruskin’s May Queen ideas at Whitelands College in London. One of Ruskin’s pupils, Mrs Hodges, became headmistress of Northill School in 1894, helping to bring the May Queen and ribbon-plaiting style into the local custom.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

That means the May Queen should not be treated as proof that the modern ceremony is an untouched survival from pagan antiquity. She is better understood as a successful Victorian and Edwardian graft onto an older local May tradition. This does not make her false. It shows how village ritual survives: by accepting new symbols when they help the custom remain meaningful, teachable and publicly attractive.

The “Moggies” preserve a rougher, more comic layer. The official Ickwell history describes the Moggies with blackened faces as traditional May figures: the sweep and his wife. Today the “chimney sweep’s wife” or “moggie” still appears and helps collect funds for the continuation of the celebrations; visitors may also see “My Lord and Lady” dressed in finery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

Calendar Customs describes the Moggies as “lucky chimney sweeps” associated with the date, appearing in the procession with the May Queen and attendants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://calendarcustoms.com/articles/ickwell-may-day/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: calendarcustoms.com">[calendarcustoms.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">calendarcustoms.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day</span></span></span> Older accounts of English May customs often include sweeps, garlands, begging, disguise, music and comic inversion. At Ickwell, those elements have been domesticated into a family festival, but they still hint at a May Day older than school-pageant neatness: noisy, playful, half-ritual and half-fundraiser.

The decorated hoops also connect old and new. Today, participating children decorate hoops with flowers and ribbon, and artificial flowers are not permitted. They bring them to The Crown at Northill for judging before the procession.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span> The rule about real flowers may look like a small practical detail, but it keeps the event tied to the season. May Day is not only represented; it is physically gathered and carried.

## How the festival changed and survived

Ickwell May Day has survived because it has never depended on one single form. Its old parish feast, its maypole, its schoolchildren, its May Queen, its adult dancers, its committee, and its visitor-friendly bank holiday format all belong to different moments in its history.

The nineteenth century was decisive. The permanent maypole was erected in 1872, and Harvey’s bequest gave the custom a financial and moral push. A bunch of mayflower, meaning hawthorn, is still tied around the pole each year before the festivities begin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span> This detail keeps the maypole from becoming merely a painted monument. It is re-seasoned annually with living greenery.

The schools were just as important as the squire. The official history says Caldecote school had a half-day holiday to watch the festivities at least from 1864, and in 1911 Caldecote was invited to send dancers. Old Warden later joined too. Today, the May Queen, attendants, flower girls, page boys and performers are drawn from the connected villages of Ickwell, Northill, Caldecote and Old Warden.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

This widened the custom from one green into a parish network. Children did not merely watch; they rehearsed, processed, danced and grew up inside the pattern. The current participant rules continue that structure: children may dance if they are of school age on 1 May and live in Caldecote, Old Warden, Ickwell, Northill, Thorncote or Hatch, or have attended Caldecote or Northill Lower School.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

The Old Scholars are perhaps the clearest sign of ritual survival. Ickwell’s official site says the festival has twenty-four adult dancers, almost all former pupils of the village school, who also plait ribbons around the maypole; many have children and grandchildren taking part on the same day.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span> Calendar Customs likewise notes that adult dancers perform alongside children, many having taken part as children themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://calendarcustoms.com/articles/ickwell-may-day/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: calendarcustoms.com">[calendarcustoms.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">calendarcustoms.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

That is the custom’s real engine. A child learns the dance, leaves school, returns as an adult, and may watch the next generation repeat the pattern. The ritual survives not because every detail is ancient, but because participation is remembered in the body.

The twentieth century brought interruptions and reorganisation. Bedfordshire Archives records that a Northill school logbook listed May Queens from 1906 to 1920, with entries from 1915 to 1919 marked as having no ceremony on the green owing to the Great War, and 1920 marked as “on Green again”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk/CommunityHistories/Ickwell/IckwellMayDay.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk">[Bedfordshire Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hosted By Bedford Borough Council: Ickwell May Day...</span></span></span> In 1945, the Ickwell and District May Day Committee was formed, and the official history says that committee still organises the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

This matters because “survival” is often misunderstood. A custom does not survive by avoiding disruption. It survives by having people, records, money, rehearsal spaces, permissions, costumes, routes, and enough local affection to restart after disruption. Ickwell’s history contains gaps, redesigns and interruptions, but also repeated acts of repair.

## What a visitor sees today

A modern visitor sees a carefully managed public event, but one that still has the bones of a village rite. The official 2026-27 information lists a programme beginning with Morris dancing at The Crown in Northill at 11.30 am, children assembling with hoops at 12.45 pm, the procession to Ickwell Green at 1.30 pm, and the crowning of the May Queen at 2 pm followed by dancing by children and adults from within the parish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

The event is now held on the early May Bank Holiday Monday rather than always on 1 May. Calendar Customs describes the location as Northill and Ickwell Green, with the procession moving from outside The Crown to the maypole arena. It also notes the practical modern visitor details: seating in the arena, stalls, refreshments, parking, toilets, and the possibility that very bad weather may delay the dancing even when the procession and crowning continue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://calendarcustoms.com/articles/ickwell-may-day/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: calendarcustoms.com">[calendarcustoms.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">calendarcustoms.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

These details may sound mundane, but they are part of why the ritual remains alive. A custom that attracts visitors must handle traffic, seating, toilets and wet grass. A custom that includes children must organise practice, eligibility and safeguarding. Ickwell May Day survives because it has allowed the ancient-looking performance to sit inside modern event management without losing its local core.

The date also shows adaptation. England’s early May bank holiday was introduced in 1978, making the first Monday in May a practical public holiday rather than requiring everyone to gather on the calendar date of 1 May.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Day" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May Day</span></span></span> Ickwell’s choice of the bank holiday helps the custom remain public, family-friendly and viable for visitors, while local May morning dancing and older associations keep the link with the traditional season.

## Why Ickwell matters to Bedfordshire folklore

Bedfordshire does not have one dominant legendary cycle in the way some counties do. Its folklore is often local, practical and place-bound: a witch-testing record here, a black dog there, a holy well, an earthwork, a Bunyan landscape, a village custom. Ickwell May Day stands out because it is a living public tradition with unusually strong local documentation and a visible annual performance.

It also complicates the easy story that folklore is simply “old stuff”. The Ickwell custom is old, but not simple. The sixteenth-century record tells us there was a substantial parish May celebration with feasting, beer, music, Morris gear and bells. The nineteenth century gave Ickwell a permanent maypole, a bequest, and eventually the May Queen and ribbon-plaiting style. The twentieth century added war interruption, revival, committee organisation and a stronger role for adult former pupils. The twenty-first century adds visitor information, access planning, social media visibility and bank holiday scheduling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk/CommunityHistories/Ickwell/IckwellMayDay.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk">[bedford.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bedfordshire Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hosted By Bedford Borough Council: Ickwell May Day...</span></span></span>

That layered history is precisely why the custom is valuable. It shows that village ritual is not a fossil. It is more like a maypole ribbon dance: strands cross, separate, tighten, loosen and form a pattern only because people keep moving.

Ickwell also shows how folklore can belong to ordinary civic life. The festival needs dancers, stewards, parents, musicians, costume-keepers, fundraisers, flower-gatherers, committee members and spectators. The “Moggie” collecting funds is not an embarrassing add-on to the ritual; it is a reminder that customs survive when communities pay for them, laugh with them and treat them as shared work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

## The survival of village ritual

The most useful way to understand Ickwell May Day is not to ask whether every part is ancient. The better question is how a Bedfordshire village custom has kept enough continuity to remain recognisable while changing enough to remain usable.

Several survival mechanisms stand out. The custom has a fixed place in Ickwell Green, a repeated route from Northill, a visible object in the maypole, named roles such as May Queen and Moggies, a training system through children’s practices, a return path through the Old Scholars, and an organising body formed after the Second World War. Each mechanism turns memory into action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

It also survives because it belongs to more than one village. Northill, Ickwell, Caldecote, Old Warden, Thorncote and Hatch all appear in the modern participation geography. That wider parish identity gives the festival a larger pool of children, families and former pupils while keeping it local enough to feel inherited rather than imported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ickwellmayday.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ickwellmayday.co.uk">[Ickwell May Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ickwellmayday.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ickwell May Day HOME &#124; Ickwell May Day</span></span></span>

For readers interested in Bedfordshire folklore, Ickwell May Day is therefore a key example of living custom. It is not a supernatural legend, but it works in a similar cultural way: it binds story to place, gives people roles to perform, and lets the past appear in the present without pretending that nothing has changed. On Ickwell Green, the old question of folklore survival has a plain answer. The village still dances because the ritual has been made practical, public and personal, year after year.

  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
</section>
