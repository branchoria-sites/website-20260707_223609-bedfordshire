## Introduction

The strongest evidence for Banffshire’s folklore comes from local history collections, antiquarian recording, oral traditions and the survival of place-based stories rather than from a single medieval legend cycle. The county’s identity has long been tied to storytelling, with organisations such as the Banffshire Field Club preserving local historical material, including printed works, newspapers, parish records and other sources useful for studying regional traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://banffshirefieldclub.org.uk/research_resources.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: banffshirefieldclub.org.uk">[banffshirefieldclub.org.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">banffshirefieldclub.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BFC &#124; Research ResourcesCollections of local studies material for Banffshire, including printed works, are held at libraries in Keith, Bu...</span></span></span>

Banffshire’s folklore matters because it shows how communities explained the unknown: ancient monuments became the work of giants or supernatural beings, lonely roads acquired ghost stories, unusual landscapes gained legendary associations, and older beliefs survived by adapting to new religious and social settings.

## The landscape behind Banffshire’s legends

### A county shaped by coast, hills and old boundaries

Historic Banffshire stretched from the Moray coast into inland valleys and mountain country, creating a varied environment for storytelling. Its geography included fishing communities, agricultural districts, river routes and upland areas connected with wider Highland traditions. The county’s boundaries changed over time, and some places associated with Banffshire history sit differently on modern administrative maps.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lordlieutenantbanffshire.co.uk/historic-county-of-banffshire" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lordlieutenantbanffshire.co.uk">[LordLieutenantBanff]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lordlieutenantbanffshire.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LordLieutenantBanffThe Historic County of BanffshireIt had two Royal Burghs, Banff and Cullen, and its main towns are Aberlour, Banff, Bu...</span></span></span>

This mixture of landscapes helped create different types of folklore. Coastal communities often developed stories around dangerous waters, storms and strange encounters, while inland areas preserved traditions connected with hills, woods, wells and ancient stones. A remote track or a particular bend in a river could become a place where a warning tale, ghost story or fairy belief was remembered.

The landscape itself often acted as a memory system. A hill was not simply a hill; it might be connected with a story of hidden treasure, a supernatural meeting or an ancient event. A stone was not only a geological object; it could become a marker of giants, saints, ancestors or forgotten people.

## Fairy traditions and the hidden landscape

Fairy belief was one of the most widespread elements of Scottish rural folklore, and Banffshire formed part of the wider north-eastern Scottish tradition of stories about otherworldly beings living alongside humans. These traditions included beliefs in fairy dwellings, dangerous encounters with fairy folk, and warnings about disturbing certain places at the wrong time.

Unlike modern fantasy portrayals, Scottish [fairy traditions]({{ 'fairy-traditions-9288b4/' | relative_url }}) were often complex and unsettling. Fairies could offer gifts or assistance, but they could also mislead travellers, steal away people, or bring illness and misfortune. In many Scottish traditions, fairy belief overlapped with ideas about healing, witchcraft and unexplained sickness. Scholars have noted that early modern Scottish witchcraft records sometimes reveal connections between fairy beliefs, supernatural explanations of illness and local healing traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249014342_Research_Article_Getting_Shot_of_Elves_Healing_Witchcraft_and_Fairies_in_the_Scottish_Witchcraft_Trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate Research Article: Getting Shot of Elves: Healing, Witchcraft</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Article: Getting Shot of Elves: Healing, Witchcraft...April 1, 2005 — This paper re-examines the evidence of the Sc...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 1, 2005</span></span></span>

Banffshire’s upland landscapes were particularly suited to these stories. Hills, caves, woods and lonely stretches of countryside provided natural settings for tales of hidden communities beneath the earth or encounters on the edge of human settlement.

The importance of these traditions today is not that they provide evidence of literal fairy beings, but that they preserve older ways of understanding relationships between people and landscape. Fairy stories often marked places where people felt caution, respect or uncertainty.

## Witches, healers and local memory

Stories of [witches]({{ 'witches-a14dc2/' | relative_url }}) in Banffshire belong to a wider Scottish tradition in which accusations of witchcraft, folk healing and supernatural explanations became closely connected. In early modern Scotland, people could be accused of harmful magic, but communities also relied on individuals believed to possess useful knowledge of herbs, charms and healing practices.

The boundary between “witch” and “healer” was often unclear in popular belief. A person with unusual knowledge could be respected in one setting and feared in another. Scottish witchcraft history shows how beliefs about magic, fairies and illness interacted rather than existing as separate traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249014342_Research_Article_Getting_Shot_of_Elves_Healing_Witchcraft_and_Fairies_in_the_Scottish_Witchcraft_Trials" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate Research Article: Getting Shot of Elves: Healing, Witchcraft</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Article: Getting Shot of Elves: Healing, Witchcraft...April 1, 2005 — This paper re-examines the evidence of the Sc...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 1, 2005</span></span></span>

Banffshire’s witch traditions are best understood through this broader social history. They reflect anxieties about illness, farming problems, storms, family disputes and unexplained events. Later retellings sometimes transformed local memories into dramatic stories, but the original traditions were usually rooted in ordinary community concerns.

## Ghosts, black dogs and haunted roads

The black dog is one of Britain’s most recognisable supernatural traditions. Across Scotland and other parts of Britain, stories describe large dark dogs appearing on lonely roads, near old buildings or in places associated with danger and death. The creature could be interpreted as an omen, a guardian or a frightening spirit depending on local tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tetzoo.com/blog/2022/8/13/legend-of-the-black-dog" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tetzoo.com">[Tetrapod Zoology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tetzoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tetrapod Zoology Legend of the Black Dog</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tetrapod ZoologyLegend of the Black DogAugust 15, 2022 — 15 Aug 2022 — The Black Dog is a creature of contradiction, stories variously de...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2022</span></span></span>

Although Banffshire does not have one universally famous black dog legend comparable with some better-known British examples, the motif fits strongly within the county’s wider tradition of supernatural storytelling. The landscape of isolated paths, old bridges, churchyards and remote countryside provided exactly the settings where such stories flourished.

Ghost stories in Banffshire also belong to a broader pattern of Scottish folklore: old houses, ruined buildings, coastal locations and historic routes often became associated with memories of past inhabitants. These stories preserved local history in imaginative form. A ghost was frequently a way of remembering a person, an injustice, a tragedy or a place that had changed.

## Ancient stones, monuments and forgotten meanings

Banffshire contains archaeological remains that connect the county with very old human activity. Across Scotland, prehistoric monuments such as standing stones and cairns have often attracted later folklore, with communities creating stories to explain features whose original purpose had been forgotten.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://rockart.scot/about-rock-art/rock-art-of-scotland/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rockart.scot">[rockart.scot]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rockart.scot</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rock Art of ScotlandCupmarks and cup-and-ring markings are occasionally found on rocks built into Neolithic and Early Bronze Age monument...</span></span></span>

A common Scottish tradition was to explain ancient stones through giants, supernatural beings or people transformed into stone. Similar traditions are recorded widely across Scotland, even though the archaeological evidence shows that many such monuments belong to prehistoric communities with purposes very different from later folklore explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitscotland.com/things-to-do/attractions/historic/standing-stones" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitscotland.com">[VisitScotland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitscotland.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">7 Standing Stones in Scotland &#124; VisitScotlandAccording to local folklore, they were giants who refused to convert to Christi...</span></span></span>

This contrast between archaeology and folklore is one of the most interesting parts of Banffshire’s heritage. A prehistoric monument may tell one story through excavation and another through local memory. Both are valuable: archaeology explores what people built, while folklore reveals how later generations understood and reimagined those places.

## Rivers, wells and sacred places

Water has long held a special place in Scottish traditional belief. Springs and wells could be viewed as places of healing, blessing or supernatural significance. Archaeological and historical studies of Scottish holy wells show that sacred water traditions have deep roots, although the precise origins of individual wells are often difficult to date.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scarf.scot/regional/higharf/early-medieval/8-6-religion-and-ritual-evidence/8-6-6-holy-wells/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scarf.scot">[scarf.scot]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scarf.scot</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">8.6.6 Holy WellsHoly wells have a long tradition in the Highlands, with speculation that they may date back into pre-Christian times (Map...</span></span></span>

In Banffshire, as elsewhere in Scotland, wells and springs formed part of a wider landscape of belief. A particular water source might be associated with a saint, a cure, a local story or a custom involving offerings and visits.

These traditions demonstrate how practical and spiritual concerns often overlapped. Clean water was essential for everyday life, but communities also attached stories and rituals to important natural resources.

## Story collectors and the preservation of tradition

Much of what is known about Scottish folklore survives because collectors, historians and local enthusiasts recorded stories that were once mainly passed on orally. County societies played an important role in gathering local information before memories disappeared.

The Banffshire Field Club, founded in 1880, became part of this tradition of local investigation and preservation. Its work reflects a wider Victorian and early modern interest in recording regional history, natural history and customs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://banffshirefieldclub.org.uk/history.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: banffshirefieldclub.org.uk">[banffshirefieldclub.org.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">banffshirefieldclub.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BFC &#124; HistoryThe Banffshire Field Club was founded in 1880 by a number of professional and business men, though among them was the shoema...</span></span></span>

These collections must be read carefully. A written account may preserve an older oral story, but it may also reflect the interests and assumptions of the person recording it. Folklore is not a fixed archive; it changes as communities retell, adapt and reinterpret stories.

## Banffshire folklore today

Modern interest in Banffshire folklore combines local history, tourism, storytelling events and renewed appreciation for traditional landscapes. Places connected with ancient monuments, coastal heritage and historic towns attract visitors interested in the stories attached to them as well as their physical history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://discovercullen.com/events/banffshire-stories-conference/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discovercullen.com">[Discover Cullen]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discovercullen.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">banffshire stories conference</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discover CullenBanffshire Stories Conference4 Oct 2025 — Rachael is an authority on the supernatural, folklore, and storytelling and has...</span></span></span>

Today, Banffshire’s legends are usually presented as cultural heritage rather than as claims that supernatural events occurred. The value of these traditions lies in what they reveal about imagination, identity and the relationship between people and place.

The county’s folklore survives because it continues to answer familiar human questions: why does this place feel different, who lived here before us, what happened on this road, and what stories does the landscape still carry?

  function buildUrl(query, reference, marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket];
    if (!cfg) return '#';
    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
