## Introduction

Glamis Castle is one of [Angus]({{ 'what-hidden-stories-shape-angus-folklore/' | relative_url }})’s most famous places where documented history and supernatural storytelling have become inseparably linked. The castle’s reputation rests on a mixture of real events, family history, 19th-century mystery writing, and long-running ghost traditions. Stories of the Monster of Glamis, the Grey Lady, Earl Beardie’s doomed card game, and other apparitions have made the castle a central landmark in Scottish haunted folklore, even though many of the tales are better understood as legends that grew around a historic place rather than proven events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-monster-of-glamis-92015626/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine The Monster of Glamis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineThe Monster of GlamisFebruary 10, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2012</span></span></span>

Located near the village of Glamis in Angus, the castle has been the ancestral home of the Earls of Strathmore and Kinghorne for centuries. Its medieval origins, grand architecture, links with important families, and later royal connections created the kind of setting in which stories about secrets, ghosts, and hidden histories naturally developed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-monster-of-glamis-92015626/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine The Monster of Glamis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineThe Monster of GlamisFebruary 10, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2012</span></span></span>

## The castle’s history and legendary reputation

Glamis Castle’s folklore is closely tied to the way people remember old buildings. Castles often become places where historical memory and imagination overlap: a family tragedy may become a ghost story, a private room may inspire rumours of a hidden secret, and a controversial figure from the past may return in folklore as a wandering spirit.

The castle’s reputation grew particularly strongly during the 19th century, when visitors and writers became fascinated by rumours of hidden chambers and unexplained events. The “mystery of Glamis” became a popular subject of discussion, helped by the secrecy surrounding the estate and the romantic fascination with ancient castles during the Victorian period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-monster-of-glamis-92015626/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine The Monster of Glamis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineThe Monster of GlamisFebruary 10, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2012</span></span></span>

This process is important in understanding Glamis folklore. The legends are not simply a collection of ghost stories added onto an old building; they are part of a wider tradition in which landscapes preserve memories of family conflicts, social fears, punishment, secrecy, and loss. The castle became a place where historical events could be retold through supernatural imagery.

## The Monster of Glamis and the hidden chamber tale

The most famous Glamis legend is the story of the “Monster of Glamis”, a mysterious figure supposedly hidden away inside the castle. Versions of the tale vary, but the common form describes a deformed child or family member who was concealed from public view in a secret room. The room was said to have been sealed, leaving generations to wonder what the family had hidden.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-monster-of-glamis-92015626/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine The Monster of Glamis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineThe Monster of GlamisFebruary 10, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2012</span></span></span>

The legend became especially popular in the 19th and early 20th centuries, when rumours about a concealed chamber and an unknown family secret spread widely. Stories claimed that only a small number of people knew the truth, adding to the atmosphere of mystery. The tale was repeated in newspapers, books, and later popular accounts of haunted Scotland.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-monster-of-glamis-92015626/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine The Monster of Glamis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineThe Monster of GlamisFebruary 10, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2012</span></span></span>

However, there is no reliable historical evidence proving that a hidden “monster” lived at Glamis. Historians and folklore writers generally treat the story as a legend built around secrecy, aristocratic privacy, and fascination with unusual births and family mysteries. The power of the story comes less from evidence of a real hidden person and more from the way it reflects older fears about difference, inheritance, and secrets behind closed doors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-monster-of-glamis-92015626/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine The Monster of Glamis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineThe Monster of GlamisFebruary 10, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2012</span></span></span>

One famous version of the legend involves visitors trying to discover the secret room by hanging towels from the castle windows and comparing the outside appearance with the number of rooms inside. The story says some windows appeared without towels, suggesting a hidden space. Such tales helped transform the castle itself into a puzzle for visitors and storytellers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://clan.com/blog/folklore-friday-the-monster-of-glamis?srsltid=AfmBOorVImiyXi-tnLTeSpwSuhSpn5EOXG2STX9oH0NAMijPu5Wx5Rsr" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: clan.com">[Clan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">clan.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folklore Friday - The Monster of Glamis - CLANA group of guests, visiting the castle, once went on a search to find the secret chamber...</span></span></span>

## Ghost stories, tragedy and haunted traditions

### The Grey Lady of Glamis

Among Glamis’s best-known ghosts is the Grey Lady, traditionally identified as Lady Janet Douglas, Lady Glamis. Her story connects folklore with a genuine historical tragedy. In 1537, Janet Douglas was accused of witchcraft and treason during the reign of James V of Scotland and was executed at Edinburgh. Later tradition transformed her memory into that of a ghostly figure associated with the castle chapel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://agupdate.com/agriview/news/regional/article_45f3b116-9ae1-11ef-a12b-e7ad25e9104e.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: agupdate.com">[AgUpdate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">agupdate.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Darkness infects Glamis CastleNov 5, 2024 — Lady Janet Douglas, called the Grey Lady Ghost, is said to haunt Glamis Castle.... The Grey Lady legend shows how Scottish ghost traditions often preserve emotional memories rather than straightforward historical accounts. The ghost story keeps alive themes of injustice, political danger, and the suffering of a powerful family caught in royal conflict.

### Earl Beardie and the Devil’s card game

Another famous Glamis tale concerns “Earl Beardie”, usually identified with Alexander Lindsay, 4th Earl of Crawford, who became associated with a story of gambling and punishment. According to legend, he played cards on the Sabbath and refused to stop, eventually playing against the Devil himself. The story says he was condemned to continue playing forever, creating a ghostly connection between Glamis Castle and moral tales about pride and temptation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://agupdate.com/agriview/news/regional/article_45f3b116-9ae1-11ef-a12b-e7ad25e9104e.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: agupdate.com">[AgUpdate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">agupdate.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Darkness infects Glamis CastleNov 5, 2024 — Lady Janet Douglas, called the Grey Lady Ghost, is said to haunt Glamis Castle.... Like many supernatural traditions attached to historic houses, the story reflects older storytelling patterns. A powerful person breaks a social or religious rule, suffers a strange punishment, and remains trapped in an endless supernatural fate.

Glamis folklore includes a number of other reported apparitions, including tales of a tongueless woman, a mysterious figure associated with the castle grounds, and strange presences within the building. These stories have developed through repeated retelling rather than through historical records, but they remain part of the castle’s modern identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://file770.com/in-search-of-the-glamis-monster/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: file770.com">[File 770]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">file770.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">in search of the glamis monster</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">File 770In Search of the Glamis MonsterJun 9, 2024 — Other ghosts include that of an unknown woman with no tongue and of a madman alleged...</span></span></span>

## Why Glamis matters in Angus folklore

Glamis Castle occupies a special place in Angus folklore because it demonstrates how a real historic site can become a centre of legendary meaning. The stories attached to the castle are not separate from its history; they have grown from the building’s age, its owners, its public image, and the fascination surrounding aristocratic houses.

The legends also show the changing nature of folklore. Older traditions about ghosts and moral warnings blended with Victorian fascination for secret rooms and later tourism focused on haunted places. The result is a living tradition that continues to change while remaining tied to the Angus landscape.

For visitors, the appeal of Glamis lies in this meeting point between fact and story. The castle’s documented history gives the legends their setting, while the legends give the historic building a powerful place in Scotland’s popular imagination. Modern tours and seasonal events continue to present these stories as part of the castle’s cultural identity, keeping the haunted traditions alive for new audiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.glamis-castle.co.uk/event/ghosts-at-glamis/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: glamis-castle.co.uk">[Glamis Castle]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">glamis-castle.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Glamis Castle Ghosts at Glamis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Glamis CastleGhosts at Glamis - Glamis CastleOctober 24, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2025</span></span></span>

## The difference between history and legend at Glamis

The enduring fascination of Glamis comes from the tension between what can be documented and what belongs to folklore. The castle’s medieval and family history can be studied through historical records, while stories of hidden rooms, ghosts, and supernatural encounters belong to the realm of tradition and storytelling.

This distinction does not make the legends unimportant. Folklore is often valuable because it reveals what communities remembered, feared, admired, or found mysterious about a place. The Monster of Glamis may not be a confirmed historical figure, and the ghosts cannot be verified as events, but the stories themselves form an important part of Angus’s cultural landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-monster-of-glamis-92015626/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine The Monster of Glamis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineThe Monster of GlamisFebruary 10, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2012</span></span></span>

Glamis Castle therefore remains one of Angus’s clearest examples of how history and folklore interact: a real place carrying centuries of human experience, surrounded by stories that continue to shape how people imagine the past.
