## Introduction

Rosslyn Chapel became [Midlothian]({{ 'where-midlothians-legends-meet-the/' | relative_url }})’s most famous “mystery” site because it offers exactly the material from which legends grow: a dramatic [Roslin Glen]({{ 'roslin-glen/' | relative_url }}) setting, a powerful founding family, dense medieval carving, unfinished architecture, a memorable craft-murder tale, and modern stories about hidden codes, Templars and the Holy Grail. The chapel is real and well documented, but many of its best-known “secrets” are better understood as layered folklore: some old local legend, some antiquarian romance, some symbolic interpretation, and some recent popular culture. Rosslyn Chapel was founded by Sir William St Clair in 1446, dedicated as the Collegiate Chapel of St Matthew in 1450, and still stands in Roslin, Midlothian, around seven miles south of Edinburgh.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/about/rosslyn-chapel-timeline/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rosslyn chapel timeline</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel WebsiteRosslyn Chapel Timeline - The Official...December 1, 2021 — 1446 William St Clair, 3rd Prince of Orkn...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2021</span></span></span>

The useful question is not whether every Rosslyn story is “true”. Folklore rarely works that way. The better question is why this one small chapel became a legend-machine. Its carvings invite close looking; its gaps invite speculation; its family history links it to older Scottish power; and *The Da Vinci Code* turned local mystery into global tourist folklore. ## Why Rosslyn became Midlothian’s legend-machine

Rosslyn Chapel belongs to Midlothian’s wider pattern of place-based folklore. The county’s strongest traditions often cling to named sites: glens, wells, rocks, ruined buildings and old routes. Rosslyn fits that pattern perfectly because the chapel is not just a building with stories attached; it is a building whose visible surfaces seem to ask for stories. The official chapel material stresses its rural Midlothian setting, ornate stonework and “mysterious symbolism” as reasons it has attracted writers, artists and visitors for generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The beauty of its setting, in rural Midlothian, the mysterious symbolism of its ornate stonework.Read more...</span></span></span>

The building’s incompleteness matters. The founder, William St Clair, died in 1484, and building work is thought to have stopped then, leaving the chapel in the form visitors see today; the Trust notes that a much larger cruciform church may once have been planned. Historic Environment Scotland’s listing also records that foundations for the whole chapel were uncovered in the nineteenth century, reinforcing the sense of a grand design partly realised and partly missing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/about/rosslyn-chapel-timeline/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rosslyn chapel timeline</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel WebsiteRosslyn Chapel Timeline - The Official...December 1, 2021 — 1446 William St Clair, 3rd Prince of Orkn...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2021</span></span></span>

That “unfinished” quality is not a minor architectural detail. It gives Rosslyn one of its most powerful folklore mechanisms: the visible building seems to point beyond itself. Visitors see a richly carved chapel that appears both complete and incomplete, Christian and strange, intimate and over-detailed. Where ordinary church sculpture might be read as decoration, Rosslyn’s density encourages puzzle-reading. It becomes easy to imagine that the building is not simply decorated, but encoded.

The chapel’s later history also fed this atmosphere. After the Reformation, its endowments were seized, its clergy were forced to resign, and in 1592 Oliver St Clair was ordered to destroy the altars, the chapel being described as a “house and monument of idolatrie”. It then ceased to be used as a house of prayer and fell into disrepair. That cycle of sacred use, abandonment, romantic rediscovery and restoration gives Rosslyn the emotional shape of a legend: loss, survival and return.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/about/rosslyn-chapel-timeline/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rosslyn chapel timeline</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel WebsiteRosslyn Chapel Timeline - The Official...December 1, 2021 — 1446 William St Clair, 3rd Prince of Orkn...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2021</span></span></span>

## The Apprentice Pillar and craft legend

The Apprentice Pillar is Rosslyn’s most famous traditional legend because it turns stone carving into a human drama. The pillar stands about eight feet high, with vines twisting around it and dragons at its base. According to the story, the master mason travelled away to study the design, leaving his apprentice behind. When the apprentice completed the pillar more beautifully than expected, the master returned, was overcome by jealousy, and killed him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/highlights/the-apprentice-pillar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website The Apprentice Pillar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website The Apprentice Pillar</span></span></span>

This is a classic craft legend. It explains an exceptional object by turning workmanship into a moral tale: talent, envy, pride, violence and punishment. The point is not that the story can be treated as a court record. Its value lies in how it helps visitors read the building. Instead of seeing the pillar only as a technical achievement, they see a scene of apprenticeship, rivalry and dangerous beauty. The carving becomes memorable because the story gives it emotional stakes.

The detail of the dragons deepens the effect. Rosslyn Chapel Trust describes eight dragons carved around the base of the pillar, linked to the St Clair family’s Norse heritage and possibly to the dragons of Niflheim guarding the great ash tree Yggdrasil. A Rosslyn educational fact sheet places the dragons at the south-east corner of the chapel and describes them as twisted around one another, biting or chewing cords that descend from the pillar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/highlights/the-apprentice-pillar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website The Apprentice Pillar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website The Apprentice Pillar</span></span></span>

For folklore readers, that matters because the pillar fuses several registers at once. It is a Christian chapel feature, a mason’s masterpiece, a family-memory object, and a story-bearing image with dragons at its root. In a county folklore frame, the Apprentice Pillar is not simply “a carving with a legend”; it is the kind of object that lets Midlothian’s past be felt through a localised tale of making, skill and fatal jealousy.

## Green Men, dragons and symbolic readings

Rosslyn’s Green Men are among its most discussed carvings. The chapel says there are around, or over, 100 Green Man carvings inside and outside the building. These faces with foliage emerging from their mouths are common in medieval churches, but Rosslyn is unusual for having so many of them. The Trust presents them as symbols of fertility and rebirth, while also noting the wider medieval association of moral possibility: the capacity for great goodness and for significant evil.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/visit/things-to-do/count-the-green-men/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website Count The Green Men</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website Count The Green Men</span></span></span>

This is where Rosslyn often gets pulled between careful interpretation and over-reading. Green Men are real medieval motifs, not modern inventions. Yet the leap from “Rosslyn has many Green Men” to “Rosslyn preserves a hidden pagan religion” is much harder to prove. The safer and more interesting reading is that Rosslyn’s carvings show how late medieval Christian buildings could absorb nature imagery, moral symbolism and older-looking motifs without needing a single secret key.

The chapel’s own material encourages visitors to look closely at the carvings but does not reduce them to one code. Its “Explore the Carvings” guide points to rural scenes, knights, Green Men, an angel playing bagpipes, the Seven Deadly Sins and the Seven Acts of Mercy. That variety is important. Rosslyn’s mystery comes partly from abundance. There is too much to take in at once, so each generation selects the carvings that suit its own questions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/visit/things-to-do/explore-the-carvings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website Explore The Carvings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website Explore The Carvings</span></span></span>

The dragon carvings perform a similar double function. They are visually striking and easy to mythologise, but they also fit late medieval decorative and moral language. Rosslyn’s educational materials treat them as carvings that can introduce children to medieval stonework, knights, dragons and natural-world symbolism. That is a more grounded approach than treating every dragon as evidence of a hidden order or coded map.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archaeologyscotland.org.uk/learn/https-archaeologyscotland-org-uk-heritage-resources-portal-rosslyn-chapel-digital-learning-resources/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyscotland.org.uk">[archaeologyscotland.org.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyscotland.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archaeologyscotland.org.uk.</span></span></span>

The most persistent symbolic trap is the assumption that strangeness equals secrecy. Rosslyn is certainly strange in the best sense: dense, inventive and visually restless. But medieval religious art often aimed to compress scripture, moral teaching, family status, local identity, craft display and wonder into one space. Rosslyn’s carvings do not need to be a conspiracy to be mysterious.

## The “code” problem: when interpretation becomes modern folklore

Rosslyn is famous for stories about codes, hidden knowledge and secret messages. Some interpretations focus on carved cubes, plant motifs, Masonic-looking gestures, possible Templar symbolism or supposed links to the Holy Grail. These claims are part of Rosslyn’s modern folklore, even when historians reject them. They matter because they show how modern visitors have learned to approach the chapel as a puzzle.

The plant carvings are a good example. Some alternative-history writers have argued that carvings at Rosslyn show maize or other New World plants, implying pre-Columbian contact with the Americas. Rosslyn’s own plant-carving page takes a much more restrained line, identifying native and exotic-looking plants such as aloe vera, ferns, curly kale and oak leaves without using them as proof of secret voyages. The broader critical literature has treated the maize claim as an example of resemblance being stretched into evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/visit/things-to-do/identify-the-plant-carvings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website Identify The Plant Carvings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website Identify The Plant Carvings</span></span></span>

The Knights Templar and Freemasonry claims are even more influential. The chapel’s family, the St Clairs or Sinclairs, has long attracted speculative attention because of Scottish noble history, masonic associations and the romance of medieval orders. Rosslyn Chapel’s own article on the Knights Templar is notably cautious, stating that many stories connect the Templars with Scotland but that there is no evidence for some of the most popular claims, including the idea that Templars fought with Robert the Bruce at Bannockburn.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/256_Rosslyn_Article_Knights_Templar.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rosslynchapel.com.</span></span></span>

Historic dating is the simplest check on many of these theories. Rosslyn Chapel was founded in 1446, more than a century after the medieval Templar order was dissolved in the early fourteenth century. That does not stop people telling Templar stories about Rosslyn, but it does change what sort of stories they are. They are not straightforward medieval traditions preserved from the time of the Templars; they are later legends, speculative histories and modern mystery folklore layered onto a medieval site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/about/rosslyn-chapel-timeline/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rosslyn chapel timeline</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel WebsiteRosslyn Chapel Timeline - The Official...December 1, 2021 — 1446 William St Clair, 3rd Prince of Orkn...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2021</span></span></span>

This distinction is not meant to drain Rosslyn of interest. It makes the site more interesting. Rosslyn shows how folklore can be generated by interpretation itself. A carving is noticed, compared, argued over, repeated in books, echoed by guides, challenged by historians, then encountered by visitors who already know the rumour. The “mystery” becomes part of the object’s public life.

## Da Vinci Code tourism and modern folklore

*The Da Vinci Code* transformed Rosslyn from a famous Scottish chapel into an international mystery destination. Dan Brown’s novel was published on 18 March 2003 and sends its characters through a Grail mystery that ends at Rosslyn Chapel. Rosslyn Chapel Trust states that the book significantly increased visitor numbers: from around 38,000 before the novel to nearly 80,000 after publication, and to more than 176,000 after the film was released.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/news/new-da-vinci-code-display-for-visitor-centre/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website New Da Vinci Code Display for Visitor Centre</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel WebsiteNew Da Vinci Code Display for Visitor Centre - Rosslyn ChapelOctober 11, 2024 — 11 Oct 2024 — The Da V...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 11, 2024</span></span></span>

This is modern folklore in action. A novel did not invent Rosslyn’s carvings, the Apprentice Pillar or the older aura of mystery, but it reorganised how many people understood them. Visitors arrived already primed to look for clues. The chapel became a real-world stage for a fictional treasure hunt, and that fictional frame fed back into tourism, interpretation, guidebooks, websites and public memory.

The effect did not vanish quickly. Research commissioned for the twenty-first anniversary of the novel found that visitors were still strongly influenced by Rosslyn’s role in *The Da Vinci Code*. The chapel welcomed 142,211 visitors in 2023 as numbers recovered after the pandemic, and in 2024 the Trust opened a new visitor-centre display about the book’s impact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/news/research-reveals-da-vinci-code-impact-21-years-after-publication/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website Research reveals Da Vinci Code impact</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Chapel welcomed 142,211 visitors, as numbers...Read more...</span></span></span>

That does not mean Rosslyn has become merely a film-location attraction. The chapel’s post-*Da Vinci Code* fame helped fund conservation, and the Trust states that the surge in visitor numbers allowed it to complete a major conservation project. This creates a useful tension: a speculative thriller popularised claims that historians dispute, yet the resulting attention helped sustain the physical building that makes older local legend possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/about/the-da-vinci-code-at-rosslyn/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website The Da Vinci Code</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website The Da Vinci Code</span></span></span>

## What is old tradition, and what is modern invention?

Rosslyn works best when its stories are sorted rather than flattened. Some traditions are attached to visible medieval fabric, some belong to later family and antiquarian storytelling, and some are recent media-driven folklore. All three can be culturally important, but they should not be treated as the same kind of evidence.

The Apprentice Pillar legend is best understood as a local craft legend attached to a real carved feature. It may not be provable as a historical murder story, but it behaves like traditional folklore: it explains a striking object, moralises craft pride and gives visitors a story they can retell. The Green Men and dragons are real carvings with medieval and symbolic contexts, though their meanings are debated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/highlights/the-apprentice-pillar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website The Apprentice Pillar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website The Apprentice Pillar</span></span></span>

The Templar, Grail and secret-code theories are different. They are part of Rosslyn’s modern mystery tradition, but they rely on speculative interpretation rather than secure medieval documentation. Rosslyn Chapel Trust and critical historians repeatedly stress the lack of firm evidence behind the strongest Templar claims. That makes them valuable as folklore and tourism history, but risky as history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/256_Rosslyn_Article_Knights_Templar.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rosslynchapel.com.</span></span></span>

The *Da Vinci Code* layer is different again. It is not old oral tradition pretending to be history; it is a known work of fiction that reshaped a real place. Its importance lies in reception. It shows how a novel can make a local site globally legible, giving millions of readers a reason to connect Roslin, Midlothian, with the Holy Grail, secret lineages and hidden knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rosslynchapel.com/news/research-reveals-da-vinci-code-impact-21-years-after-publication/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosslynchapel.com">[The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosslynchapel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Official Rosslyn Chapel Website Research reveals Da Vinci Code impact</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Chapel welcomed 142,211 visitors, as numbers...Read more...</span></span></span>

A grounded way to read Rosslyn is to ask three questions of any claim:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Is it attached to a visible feature of the chapel, such as the Apprentice Pillar or Green Men?
* Is it supported by historical records, or mainly by later interpretation?
* Did it become popular through modern media, tourism and repetition?

</div>

This approach keeps the wonder without surrendering judgement. Rosslyn does not become less fascinating when weak claims are separated from strong ones. It becomes clearer why the chapel has been able to carry so many different kinds of story.

## Why Rosslyn matters in Midlothian folklore

Rosslyn Chapel matters to Midlothian because it is the county’s clearest example of folklore as an ongoing process rather than a sealed archive. It shows how medieval architecture can generate legend, how family history can invite symbolic reading, how a glen setting can intensify atmosphere, and how modern fiction can turn a local monument into a global mystery landmark.

Its place in Roslin Glen is part of the spell. The chapel is close to Rosslyn Castle and the wooded North Esk landscape, making it easy for visitors to experience it not as an isolated museum object but as part of a charged Midlothian route of chapel, castle, river and ruin. Recent walking coverage still presents the area as a compact journey through natural and historic drama, with *The Da Vinci Code* adding a modern layer to an already storied landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thetimes.co.uk/article/walk-of-the-week-woodland-wander-castle-midlothian-696nnb30c" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thetimes.co.uk">[The Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thetimes.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Times Walk of the week: woodland wander to a storied castle in Midlothian</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Start at Roslin Glen Country Park car park, then navigate through paths leading to Rosslyn Castle ruins and Rosslyn Chapel. For county folklore, Rosslyn also offers a useful caution. Famous places attract claims. Some claims preserve local imagination; others market mystery too aggressively; others are sincere but poorly evidenced attempts to decode the past. A good Midlothian folklore page should allow Rosslyn to be magical without pretending that every rumour is medieval truth.

That balance is what makes Rosslyn endure. The chapel is not just a backdrop for conspiracy theories, and it is not just a beautiful church stripped of legend. It is a rare place where stone carving, craft tale, Christian symbolism, Norse-flavoured dragon imagery, romantic ruin culture, conservation, tourism and modern thriller fiction have all met in one Midlothian building. Rosslyn Chapel became a legend-machine because it gives every generation something to interpret, argue over and retell.
