## Introduction

The historic county should not be confused entirely with the modern council area of Argyll and Bute. Modern boundaries combine several historic regions, while the older county identity remains important for folklore because stories often follow islands, parishes, estates, and traditional travel routes rather than administrative borders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.argyll-bute.gov.uk/my-community/local-history-and-heritage/history-argyll-and-bute" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: argyll-bute.gov.uk">[Argyll and Bute Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">argyll-bute.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Argyll and Bute Council The History of Argyll and Bute &#124; Argyll and Bute Council</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Argyll and Bute Council The History of Argyll and Bute &#124; Argyll and Bute Council</span></span></span>

## The Island Landscape Behind Buteshire Folklore

### Why islands created powerful traditions

Buteshire’s geography encouraged storytelling. Islands such as Bute and Arran were places where communities lived closely with the sea, weather, farming cycles, and ancient monuments. In such environments, unusual rocks, old burial places, ruined buildings, and dangerous stretches of water often became attached to legends explaining their origins or warning people how to behave.

The wider Argyll and Bute region contains a dense archaeological landscape of prehistoric remains, including standing stones and other ancient monuments. These places were not simply abandoned relics in local memory: later generations often reinterpreted them through stories of giants, spirits, saints, or supernatural events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.argyll-bute.gov.uk/my-community/local-history-and-heritage/history-argyll-and-bute" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: argyll-bute.gov.uk">[Argyll and Bute Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">argyll-bute.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Argyll and Bute Council The History of Argyll and Bute &#124; Argyll and Bute Council</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Argyll and Bute Council The History of Argyll and Bute &#124; Argyll and Bute Council</span></span></span>

Bute’s position between mainland Scotland and the western seaways also placed it within a wider Gaelic storytelling world. Folklore from this region shares themes found elsewhere in western Scotland: fairy encounters, warnings about strange places after dark, supernatural explanations for illness or misfortune, and memories of saints connected with springs and churches.

## The Bute Witches and the Fear of Hidden Powers

One of Buteshire’s most historically documented pieces of folklore is the story of the Bute witch trials. During the Scottish witch-hunting period of the 17th century, women from the parish of Rothesay were accused of witchcraft. Several were tried and executed in 1662, and the surviving records show how accusations could combine ideas about harmful magic, community suspicion, and older beliefs about supernatural beings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://folklorefinder.uk/legends/the-bute-witches" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folklorefinder.uk">[Folklore Finder]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folklorefinder.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Folklore Finder The Bute Witches — Folklore Finder</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folklore Finder The Bute Witches — Folklore Finder</span></span></span>

The Bute cases are especially interesting because fairy belief appears alongside witchcraft accusations. One accused woman, Margaret McWilliam, became associated in local tradition with claims that she possessed a fairy companion. Such stories show that early modern Scottish communities did not always separate “witchcraft” and fairy belief in the way modern readers might. A person accused of supernatural activity might be imagined as someone who had dealings with hidden forces, whether those forces were described as fairies, spirits, or magic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://folklorefinder.uk/legends/the-bute-witches" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folklorefinder.uk">[Folklore Finder]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folklorefinder.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Folklore Finder The Bute Witches — Folklore Finder</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folklore Finder The Bute Witches — Folklore Finder</span></span></span>

These stories are important not because they prove supernatural events occurred, but because they reveal real fears and beliefs within island communities. The witch trials preserve evidence of how neighbours interpreted illness, conflict, strange behaviour, and unexplained events through the language of their own time.

## Fairies, Spirits, and Enchanted Places

### Fairy traditions in a sea-going landscape

Fairy belief formed part of the wider folklore world of western Scotland, including island communities around Bute and Argyll. Stories of fairies were often connected with particular landscapes: hills, caves, lonely roads, shorelines, and places where ordinary rules were believed to change.

Unlike modern fantasy portrayals, Scottish [fairy traditions]({{ 'fairy-traditions/' | relative_url }}) often described these beings as unpredictable. They might offer help, but they could also bring danger, especially if humans disturbed their places, broke traditional rules, or behaved disrespectfully. This made fairy stories a way of explaining the relationship between people and the landscape.

Buteshire’s witch traditions show how closely fairy ideas could overlap with other forms of folk belief. The same community might understand a mysterious event through several possible explanations: a fairy influence, a curse, a witch’s action, or divine judgement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://folklorefinder.uk/legends/the-bute-witches" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folklorefinder.uk">[Folklore Finder]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folklorefinder.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Folklore Finder The Bute Witches — Folklore Finder</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Folklore Finder The Bute Witches — Folklore Finder</span></span></span>

## Stones, Wells, and Sacred Landscapes

### Ancient monuments and later legends

Standing stones and prehistoric sites across western Scotland have long attracted stories about their origins and meanings. In many parts of Britain, communities explained ancient stones through legends of giants, witches turned to stone, saints, or supernatural builders. Buteshire’s ancient landscape belongs to this wider tradition, where archaeological remains became part of living folklore rather than simply objects of the distant past.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.argyll-bute.gov.uk/my-community/local-history-and-heritage/history-argyll-and-bute" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: argyll-bute.gov.uk">[Argyll and Bute Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">argyll-bute.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Argyll and Bute Council The History of Argyll and Bute &#124; Argyll and Bute Council</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Argyll and Bute Council The History of Argyll and Bute &#124; Argyll and Bute Council</span></span></span>

The significance of these places changed over time. A prehistoric monument might first have had a ritual purpose, later become associated with Christian stories, and eventually become a landmark remembered through local tales. This layering of meanings is one of the defining features of Scottish folklore landscapes.

Holy wells and sacred springs also belong to this pattern. Across Scotland, springs were often linked with healing, saints, and protective customs. While specific Buteshire well traditions are less extensively recorded than some mainland examples, the region’s religious history and island landscapes contain the same tradition of treating certain natural places as spiritually significant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.argyll-bute.gov.uk/my-community/local-history-and-heritage/history-argyll-and-bute" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: argyll-bute.gov.uk">[Argyll and Bute Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">argyll-bute.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Argyll and Bute Council The History of Argyll and Bute &#124; Argyll and Bute Council</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Argyll and Bute Council The History of Argyll and Bute &#124; Argyll and Bute Council</span></span></span>

## Saints, Christianity, and Older Beliefs

Buteshire folklore also reflects the long interaction between Christianity and older landscape traditions. The western Scottish islands preserve memories of early Christian activity, saints, chapels, and sacred sites, particularly through the wider history of Argyll and the neighbouring islands.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.argyll-bute.gov.uk/my-community/local-history-and-heritage/history-argyll-and-bute" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: argyll-bute.gov.uk">[Argyll and Bute Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">argyll-bute.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Argyll and Bute Council The History of Argyll and Bute &#124; Argyll and Bute Council</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Argyll and Bute Council The History of Argyll and Bute &#124; Argyll and Bute Council</span></span></span>

Local religious traditions often absorbed earlier ideas about special places. A spring could become associated with a saint; an unusual stone could gain a Christian explanation; an ancient site could be remembered through stories of holy people. Rather than replacing older beliefs completely, Christianity often reshaped the way communities understood the landscape.

This mixture explains why folklore in Buteshire can feel layered: a single place may carry archaeological history, medieval religious memory, clan associations, and later tales of supernatural events.

## How Buteshire Folklore Is Remembered Today

Modern interest in Buteshire folklore comes through heritage organisations, museums, local history projects, walking routes, and tourism connected with the islands. The region’s historical collections and archives continue to support research into local communities, traditions, and landscapes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.argyll-bute.gov.uk/argyllcollection/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: argyll-bute.gov.uk">[Argyll and Bute Council]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">argyll-bute.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Argyll and Bute Council The Argyll Collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Argyll and Bute Council The Argyll Collection</span></span></span>

Contemporary retellings often focus on the most dramatic stories, especially the [Bute witches]({{ 'bute-witches/' | relative_url }}), ancient stones, and mysterious island locations. However, the deeper importance of the folklore lies in how ordinary people used stories to understand their surroundings. Legends preserved memories of danger, faith, social tensions, and respect for places that seemed older than human life.

Buteshire’s folklore is therefore not simply a collection of ghost stories. It is a record of how island communities imagined their relationship with the sea, the land, the past, and the unseen world.

    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
